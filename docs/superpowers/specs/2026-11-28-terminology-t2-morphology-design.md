# Terminology T2: morphology-aware matching

Date: 2026-11-28. Issue: #206 (part of #150). Roadmap:
[`docs/terminology-roadmap.md`](../../terminology-roadmap.md).
Follow-up issues created with this design: #227, #228, #229, #230.

## 1. Intent

The checker matches a term only in the exact wording that was entered. A
forbidden "Datei" does not find "Dateien", a forbidden "file" does not
find "filed", and nothing is found inside "Dateiname". A writer who wants
these caught has to enter every form as a term of its own.

T2 lets the writer switch on, per term, the matching of inflected forms
and, for German, the matching inside compounds. Because the result depends
on the part of speech and on what the language pipeline knows about a
word, the term editor shows what the system recognises and lets the writer
test a term on an example sentence. Where an inflected form is found, the
suggested replacement is inflected too when that can be done reliably,
and marked as a base form when it cannot.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**

- Two switches per term, stored, logged and carried by move and merge:
  "also match inflected forms" and "also match inside compounds".
- Inflected forms of a switched-on forbidden or deprecated term are found
  in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian and Japanese.
- For German, a switched-on term is found inside compounds.
- The term editor suggests the part of speech, points out a term that is
  not in its base form, tests the term on its example sentence, and asks
  for an example sentence where it cannot determine the part of speech
  reliably.
- For English and Japanese the suggested replacement has the form of the
  word it replaces. For the other four languages it is the base form,
  marked as such, with the existing "Suggest fix" action next to it.
- A check without switched-on terms costs what it costs today.

**Non-goals** (each is deferred, not rejected)

- Japanese compounds (#227) and Japanese spelling variants (#228).
- A check of the LLM's replacement against the termbase, and agreement of
  the surrounding words such as article and adjective endings (#229).
- Moving exact and casing matching for the western languages onto token
  matching, which also removes the cost per term of the exact path and
  fixes terms that end in a symbol (#230).
- Phrases whose words are reordered or separated in the sentence. This
  affects German most: "in Betrieb nehmen" in "nahm die Anlage in
  Betrieb", and separable verbs such as "anmelden" in "meldet sich an".
- A table of explicit forms per term for cases the pipeline gets wrong
  (reserved in the T1 design, 5.6).
- Chinese. It has no inflection.
- Inflection in the casing check for preferred and admitted terms.

**Anti-goals**

- Any change in the findings for a term that has neither switch on.
- A generated replacement that is offered for one-click use and is wrong.
  The generators decline instead of guessing.
- A part of speech guessed from a lone word and shown as a fact.
- A dependency installed from a package name whose origin is not
  verified, or under a licence that does not fit this MIT project.

## 3. Constraints

- One schema definition serves SQLite and Postgres. Columns are added
  with `migrate_columns` and checked with `verify_schema`
  (`backend/app/services/db/__init__.py`); with `manage_schema=false` the
  app only verifies.
- Pipelines are loaded lazily per language by `NlpRegistry`
  (`backend/app/nlp/registry.py`). A language whose model is missing must
  keep working with exact matching.
- The checker runs once per domain, in the order of the request, and a
  later domain's overlapping finding is dropped (T1 design, section 8).
  This stays as it is.
- A finding is consumed by the web app, the browser extension and the
  embeddable clients. Changes to its shape are additive only.
- `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` is generated by `scripts/collect-licenses.py`;
  CI fails when it is out of date.
- User-facing text follows the informal register in all seven locales
  (`frontend/src/i18n/`, pinned by `register.test.ts`).

## 4. Approaches

### 4.1 Matching

1. **An additional base-form pass (chosen).** Exact and casing matching
   stay untouched. Terms with the switch on get a second pass that
   compares base forms on the analysed document. The token matching that
   Japanese and Chinese use today is generalised into one shared function,
   which this pass uses for all six languages.
2. **All matching on tokens.** One notion of a word and one code path, and
   it fixes two existing defects (terms ending in a symbol are never found
   when a space follows; the exact path scans the text once per term). It
   also changes what existing terms match: on 32 test cases, parts of
   hyphenated words were lost in German and Spanish ("Handy" in
   "Handy-Vertrag"), and the term "can not" started to match "cannot".
   This is where the checker should end up, but it changes behaviour for
   every existing term and needs a decision per class of difference.
   Deferred to #230, which the shared function from approach 1 prepares.
3. **A dictionary lemmatizer without the tagger.** Fast, but a new
   dependency that ignores context and duplicates the loaded pipelines.

Generating all inflected forms when a term is saved and matching them as
exact strings is not possible: no reliable generator exists for German,
French, Spanish and Italian.

### 4.2 German compounds

1. **Confirm with the spelling dictionary (chosen).** A word that contains
   a switched-on term is a compound of it when what stands before and
   after the term is itself a word according to the German Hunspell
   dictionary that the backend already ships
   (`backend/dictionaries/de.*`, read with `spylls` in
   `backend/app/checkers/llm/vetting.py`). No new dependency.
2. **CharSplit**, as named in the issue. The original
   (`github.com/dtuggener/CharSplit`) is MIT-licensed, has not changed
   since April 2020 and loads a 40 MB data file. It is not published on
   PyPI by its author: the PyPI name `charsplit` belongs to an unrelated
   package first uploaded in July 2026, and `compound-split` is a fork
   under GPL-3.0. It would have to be installed from a pinned git commit.

Both were measured on two lists of term and word pairs: a first list of
42, on which the dictionary rule in 6.5 was developed, and a second list
of 60 that was written before either method was run on it. CharSplit
(commit `9710624`) was tried in three ways; the table shows its best
result per list.

| | Dictionary rule | CharSplit |
|---|---|---|
| First list, 42 pairs | 40 correct | 36 correct |
| Second list, 60 pairs | 55 correct | 49 correct |
| Compounds missed, both lists | 0 | 4 and 5 |
| Time per word | 0.2 to 0.3 ms | 0.01 to 0.03 ms |

The dictionary rule misses no compound. Its seven errors are false hits:
two words with two valid readings ("Staubecken", "Druckerzeugnis"), four
derivations with a prefix ("Aufgabe" for "Gabe", "Eingang", "Übersicht",
"Vorschlag") and "Legende" for "Ende". CharSplit reports most of the same
false hits and misses compounds as well ("Schreibtisch", "Werkzeug",
"Netzwerkkabel").

### 4.3 Replacements

1. **Mechanical rules for English and Japanese, LLM for the rest
   (chosen).** English needs a rule set with exception lists, Japanese a
   table of conjugation classes, which the tokenizer reports. For German,
   French, Spanish and Italian the sidebar offers the existing "Suggest
   fix" action, which already rewrites a span through an LLM and vets the
   result (`backend/app/api/suggestions.py`).
2. **Mechanical rules for all languages.** No maintained generator for
   German; lookup tables fail on domain terms, borrowings and product
   names; agreement of the surrounding words stays unsolved.
3. **LLM for all languages.** Spends credits where a rule is exact.

### 4.4 Part of speech in the editor

Measured on the installed pipelines:

| Source | Result |
|---|---|
| The term inside its example sentence | Correct in every case tried, in all languages |
| Dictionary | Japanese: reliable, also for recent words. English: lists every part of speech of a word ("file": noun, verb). French: usable. Spanish: small (8,000 nouns). German, Italian: none |
| The lone word | Often wrong: French "fichier" read as a verb, Spanish "archivo" as an adjective, Italian "file" as an adjective, German "Essen" as a proper noun |

Chosen: dictionary and example sentence. The reading of a lone word is
not shown, with one exception that depends on a measurement (8.3).

## 5. Data model

Two columns on `concept_terms`, both `INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `match_inflections` | Inflected forms of this term are matched. |
| `match_compounds` | This term is matched inside compounds. |

- Both are fields of `ConceptTerm` and `TermInput`, are returned and
  accepted by the concept endpoints, and default to false. Existing terms
  therefore behave as before.
- A change to either is recorded in the change log like a change to
  `case_sensitive` (`backend/app/services/terminology/changes.py`).
- A term that is moved or merged keeps both values.
- Seeding and the import tool write false.
- The values are stored as sent for every language and usage status. They
  take effect only where the table below says so, so that support for a
  further language needs no migration.

| Language | Inflected forms | Compounds |
|---|---|---|
| English, French, Spanish, Italian | yes | no |
| German | yes | yes |
| Japanese | yes | no (#227) |
| Chinese | no | no |

Both switches act on enforced terms whose usage status is forbidden or
deprecated. On a preferred or admitted term they have no effect.

## 6. Checker

### 6.1 Passes

For a domain and a language the checker runs, in this order:

1. **Exact pass and casing pass**, unchanged, for all terms.
2. **Base-form pass**, for terms with `match_inflections`, in the six
   languages of the table above.
3. **Compound pass**, for German terms with `match_compounds`.

Passes 2 and 3 are skipped when the domain has no term that qualifies.

### 6.2 The analysed document

- The base-form pass for the five western languages needs the document
  analysed by the language pipeline (base form and part of speech per
  token, and the head of a phrase). The rules checker already produces
  this document in the same request (`backend/app/api/checks.py`). It is
  handed to the terminology checker instead of being built again.
- When the rules checker did not run, the terminology checker requests
  the analysis itself, once per request and only if a selected domain has
  a qualifying term in the language. One holder per request serves the
  rules checker and all domains, so the text is never analysed twice.
- Japanese needs no analysis: its tokenizer already reports base form,
  part of speech and conjugation form. The base-form pass uses the
  tokenizer-only document there, as the exact pass does today.
- When the language has no usable pipeline, the base-form pass is
  skipped. The exact pass works as today. The editor tells the writer
  (8.2).

### 6.3 Base-form pass

- **Shared token function.** The loop in `_check_cjk` that registers
  terms in one matcher and walks the document once is extracted into a
  function that takes the document, the terms and the token attribute to
  compare. Japanese and Chinese exact and casing matching keep using it
  with the attributes they use today. The base-form pass uses it with the
  base form.
- **One pass for all terms.** The document is walked once per domain,
  whatever the number of terms. Measured: 0.02 to 0.03 ms for a document
  of 660 tokens with 100, 1,000 and 10,000 registered terms.
- **Base forms of a term.** A term is compared through a sequence of base
  forms, one per token. Up to three sequences are registered for a term,
  where they differ: the term's tokens as written; the term analysed on
  its own, unless the term has a part of speech and that analysis reads
  its head as a different one (the Spanish noun "cuenta" is read as a
  form of the verb "contar" when it stands alone); and, if the term's
  example sentence (`context`) contains the term as written, the base
  forms of that occurrence.
- **Cache.** Analysing a term costs about 0.17 ms. The sequences are kept
  in memory, keyed on language, pipeline model name, term text and
  example sentence, with an upper bound on the number of entries. The
  cache is per process. Nothing is stored in the database, so nothing
  goes stale when a model changes.
- **Multi-word terms** are compared token by token: "parts of speech"
  has the base forms of "part of speech", "virtuellen Maschinen" those of
  "virtuelle Maschine". Measured in all five western languages.
- **Punctuation and symbols.** A token without a letter or digit is
  compared as written, because the pipelines give all such tokens the
  same base form.
- **Case.** Base forms are compared without regard to case. For a term
  with `case_sensitive`, the first letter of the hit must have the case of
  the term's first letter, with the existing sentence-start exception for
  a lower-case term.
- **Part of speech.** If the term has a part of speech, the head of the
  hit must fit it; without one, any part of speech matches. The head of a
  multi-word hit is the token whose syntactic head lies outside the hit;
  in Japanese it is the last token.

  | Part of speech of the term | Accepted tags of the head |
  |---|---|
  | noun, proper noun | noun or proper noun |
  | verb | verb or auxiliary |
  | adjective | adjective; in German also adverb, because the pipeline tags a predicative adjective as an adverb |
  | adverb | adverb |
  | other | any |

- **Accepted wordings.** A hit whose text equals an enforced preferred or
  admitted term of the same domain and language is not reported. The set
  spans all concepts of the domain, not only the hit's own language
  section, so it is built per domain and language; the exact pass keeps
  its per-section set. The comparison ignores case unless the accepted
  term has `case_sensitive`. Example: "Datum" is forbidden with the
  switch on and "Daten" is preferred in another concept; "Daten" has the
  base form "Datum" and must not be flagged.
- **Overlaps within a domain.** For the same term, an exact hit wins over
  an overlapping base-form hit, and both win over a compound hit.
  Afterwards all status findings of the three passes go through the
  existing resolution (`_resolve_overlaps`): the more severe finding
  wins. One rule is added for equal severity: a finding from an earlier
  pass wins over an overlapping finding from a later pass, so an exact
  hit of one term hides a base-form hit of another on the same words.
  Two findings of the same pass and severity are both kept, as today.
  Between domains nothing changes (`drop_duplicates`).

### 6.4 Finding for an inflected hit

Severity, rule id, message and replacements follow the T1 design (section
8). `M` in "Use … instead of 'M'" is the text as it stands in the document,
so the inflected form. The suggestion is described in section 7.

### 6.5 Compound pass (German)

A candidate is a word of the text, or one part of a hyphenated word, that
contains the text of a switched-on term and is longer than it. Only
single-word terms take part. The candidate is checked as written and in
its base form where the document is analysed.

It is a compound of the term when all of the following hold:

- What stands **before** the term is empty, or is a word once a linking
  element at its end is removed.
- What stands **after** the term is empty, is only a linking element, or
  is a word once a linking element at its start is removed.
- At least one of the two is a word.
- A term that ends in "e" and has more than four letters may also occur
  without that "e", if a word follows ("Schule" in "Schulbuch", "Hilfe"
  in "Hilfsmittel").

"A word" means at least three letters and accepted by the German spelling
dictionary when capitalised. Linking elements are "s", "es", "n", "en",
"e", "er" and "ens". The term is searched in the word without regard to
case, and `case_sensitive` is ignored in this pass, because a term inside
a compound loses its capital letter.

| Word | Term | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Handyvertrag, Handyverträge | Handy | compound |
| Konfigurationsdateien | Datei | compound |
| Arbeitsspeicherverwaltung | Speicher | compound (middle part) |
| Kundennummer | Kunde | compound |
| Motor | Tor | not a compound |
| Zeitung, Tagung | Zeit, Tag | not a compound |
| Bauer, Rinde | Bau, Rind | not a compound (only an ending follows) |
| Staubecken | Staub | reported, although "Stau-Becken" is meant: accepted limit |
| Druckerzeugnis | Drucker | reported, although "Druck-Erzeugnis" is meant: accepted limit |
| Aufgabe, Eingang, Vorschlag, Übersicht | Gabe, Gang, Schlag, Sicht | reported, although these are derivations with a prefix: accepted limit |
| Legende | Ende | reported ("Leg" passes as a word): accepted limit |
| Handyverträgen | Vertrag | missed (plural with umlaut): accepted limit |

Prefixes such as "auf", "ein", "vor" and "über" are not excluded, because
the same prefixes form compounds a writer does want reported
("Unterordner" for "Ordner", "Vorname" for "Name"). The switch is per
term, and the writer sees every hit.

The finding covers the whole word. Its message is "'W' contains 'T'." and,
if the section has replacements, "Use 'A' instead.", followed by status
reason and definition as in T1. It carries no suggestion, because
replacing the whole word with the preferred term would be wrong. Severity
and rule id are those of the term. The rules about accepted wordings and
overlaps (6.3) apply, with the whole word as the hit.

The pass needs the dictionary but no pipeline. Without `de.dic` it is
skipped and the editor says so. The dictionary cache in `vetting.py` is
moved to a place both users share. The cost must not grow with the number
of switched-on terms times the length of the text; how candidates are
found in one pass is left to the builder.

### 6.6 A new field on the finding

`Finding` gains `suggestions_base_form: bool = False`. It is true when the
suggestions are base forms that do not fit the sentence as they stand
(7.3). Older clients ignore it.

## 7. Replacements

### 7.1 Common rules

- **Part of speech of the replacement.** It is the replacement term's
  stored part of speech. If none is stored, the replacement is taken to
  have the part of speech of the hit, since both name the same concept.
  If the stored value differs from the part of speech of the hit, the
  generator declines.
- **A span that grows** (7.2, 7.3) does so only if the added word is not
  part of another finding of the same domain; otherwise the generator
  declines. The message keeps quoting the hit without the added word.
- **Round trip.** The generated form is analysed again and must give back
  the replacement's base form. If it does not, the generator declines.
- When a generator declines, the suggestion is the base form and
  `suggestions_base_form` is true.
- A hit that already is the base form gets the base form, unmarked, as
  today.
- A capital first letter of the hit is carried over to the suggestion.
- With several replacements (T1: the admitted terms when no preferred
  term is active) each is generated on its own; the flag is true if any
  of them is a base form.

### 7.2 English

- The form of the hit is read from its tag: plural, past tense, past
  participle, present participle, third person singular, comparative,
  superlative.
- **Multi-word replacement:** only the head is inflected ("parts of
  speech", "virtual machines", "logged in"). The head is taken from the
  analysis of the term on its own. If that analysis does not yield a
  noun, verb or adjective as head, the generator declines.
- **Article.** If "a" or "an" stands directly before the hit and the
  replacement needs the other one, the finding's span and its suggestion
  both include the article ("a file" becomes "an archive").
- **Source of the forms.** Candidate: the library LemmInflect (MIT, last
  release October 2022, depends only on numpy). The first step of the
  second pull request checks that it installs and works with the pinned
  spaCy and numpy versions. If it does not, regular rules with exception
  lists are written in the project.

### 7.3 Japanese

- Nouns, and the noun part of a する verb, do not inflect: the suggestion
  is the replacement as stored, unmarked.
- For a verb or an い adjective, the tokenizer reports class and form of
  the hit (for example "五段-マ行;連用形-撥音便"). The same form is
  generated for the last token of the replacement, from a table that
  covers the 五段 rows, 上一段 and 下一段, する, 来る and い adjectives.
- **Following particle.** When the form of the hit and of the replacement
  differ in voicing, the token after the hit changes as well (て and で,
  た and だ, たり and だり, たら and だら). The finding's span and its
  suggestion then include that token ("読み込んで" becomes "書いて").
- The generator declines when the class of the replacement is not in the
  table, or when the replacement does not end in a token that inflects in
  the way the hit does.

### 7.4 German, French, Spanish, Italian

The suggestion is the base form with `suggestions_base_form` true.

### 7.5 Sidebar

- A finding with `suggestions_base_form` shows its suggestion buttons as
  today, a note that these are base forms whose ending has to be adjusted,
  and the existing "Suggest fix" action. Replacements fetched through
  that action are shown in addition to the base forms.
- A finding without a suggestion, such as a compound hit, shows "Suggest
  fix" as any such finding does today. No change is needed for it.
- The request sent by "Suggest fix" is unchanged: span, finding message,
  language, rule id.
- "Suggest fix" uses fetched replacements only for a finding without
  suggestions of its own (`frontend/src/findings/suggestions.ts`). For a
  finding with `suggestions_base_form` both are shown.
- **Gate before this is built.** A live test with five sentences each in
  German, French, Spanish and Italian shows whether the existing prompt
  returns the correctly inflected preferred term. If a language fails,
  the message of a finding with `suggestions_base_form` is extended by a
  sentence asking for the form that fits the sentence. The test and its
  result are recorded in the pull request.
- This part ships in the first pull request, so that a base-form
  suggestion is never shown without its note (section 12).

## 8. Term check in the editor

### 8.1 Endpoint

`POST /api/terminology/term-check`, for any signed-in user. It reads no
stored data and changes nothing. The request holds the draft of one term:
language, text, part of speech (optional), example sentence (optional),
usage status and the two switches. The response holds:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| `recognised` | Parts of speech the system recognises for the term, each with its source: `dictionary`, `example` or `phrase` |
| `known` | Whether the dictionary contains the word; absent where the language has no dictionary that can say so |
| `base_form` | The base form, if the term text is recognised as an inflected form of another word |
| `example` | For a given example sentence: whether the term is found in it by the checker's own matching with both switches on, and if so the matched text, its part of speech and its base form |
| `needs_example` | True when the part of speech cannot be determined reliably and no example sentence settles it |
| `inflections` | `supported`, `no_model` (the pipeline is not installed) or `unsupported` (language) |
| `compounds` | `supported`, `no_dictionary` or `unsupported` |
| `replacement` | For a preferred or admitted term in English or Japanese: whether inflected forms of it can be generated (second pull request) |

Term text and example sentence are limited in length; the limits are left
to the builder.

### 8.2 Sources per language

| Language | Dictionary | `known` reported | Without example sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japanese | tokenizer dictionary | yes | suggestion from the dictionary |
| English | word list of the lemmatizer, all parts of speech | yes | suggestion from the dictionary |
| French | word list of the lemmatizer | yes | suggestion from the dictionary |
| Spanish | word list of the lemmatizer, used when it contains the word | no, the list is too small | suggestion if listed, otherwise `needs_example` |
| German, Italian | none | no | `needs_example`; for German see 8.3 |

- **Where the dictionaries are.** For English, French and Spanish it is
  the table `lemma_index` of the pipeline's lemmatizer
  (`nlp.get_pipe("lemmatizer").lookups`), a set of base forms per part of
  speech. It ships inside the installed models (`lookups.bin`); no
  further package is needed. For Japanese it is the tokenizer's own
  dictionary: a term is known when it is one token that the dictionary
  tokenizer (`nlp.tokenizer.tokenizer`) does not mark as out of
  vocabulary, and its part of speech is the one the tokenizer reports.
- A dictionary yields noun, verb, adjective or adverb; never proper noun.
- **Multi-word term:** the dictionary does not apply. The term is
  analysed on its own and the part of speech of its head is offered with
  the source `phrase`, together with `needs_example`.
- **Example sentence.** It is the term's existing `context` field. When
  it contains the term, the part of speech found there is offered with
  the source `example` and takes precedence in the display.
- **Base form.** Reported when the term text is not listed as a base form
  but is recognised as a form of a listed word (Japanese, English,
  French, Spanish). For German and Italian see 8.3.

### 8.3 German and Italian without an example sentence

Whether the reading of a lone word is good enough is measured before the
editor part is built, on 200 common terms per language with their part of
speech (nouns, verbs, adjectives; the list is part of the tests). If at
least 95 % are read correctly in a language, that reading is offered as a
suggestion with the source `phrase` and the base-form hint is given from
it, both together with `needs_example`. Below 95 % the language only
gets `needs_example`. The result is recorded in the pull request.

### 8.4 Screen

In the term details (`frontend/src/terminology/LanguageSectionEditor.tsx`):

```
Part of speech  [ — ▾]      Recognised as noun or verb.  [noun] [verb]
Example         [We filed the report on Monday.]
                Found in your example as a verb (base form "file").
[ ] Match case
[x] Also match inflected forms
[ ] Also match inside compounds            (German only)
```

- The switch for inflected forms is shown for every language except
  Chinese, the switch for compounds for German only.
- The field labelled "Context" today is relabelled "Example sentence",
  so that the hints and the field use the same words. The stored field
  stays `context`.
- Each recognised part of speech is a button that sets the selection.
- The check runs when the details are open and the term text, the part of
  speech, the example or a switch changes, after a short pause in typing.
- The messages are advice. They never block saving.

| Situation | Message (English wording; the seven locales are left to the builder) |
|---|---|
| One or several parts of speech recognised, none selected | "Recognised as noun or verb." with one button each |
| Selection is not among the recognised ones | "'X' is not known as a verb. Inflected forms of that kind may be missed. Add an example sentence to test it." |
| `known` is false | "'X' is not in the dictionary. Inflected forms are recognised by rule and may be missed." |
| `base_form` present | "'Dateien' looks like an inflected form of 'Datei'." |
| `needs_example` | "Add an example sentence so that the part of speech can be determined." |
| Example given, term found | "Found in your example as a verb (base form 'file')." |
| Example given, term not found | "Not found in your example sentence." |
| `inflections` is `no_model` | "Inflected forms cannot be matched: the language model is not installed." |
| `compounds` is `no_dictionary` | "Compounds cannot be matched: the German dictionary is not installed." |
| Switch on, usage status preferred or admitted | "This only has an effect on forbidden and deprecated terms." |
| `replacement` says no forms can be generated (English, Japanese) | "Replacements with this term are offered in the base form." |

## 9. Performance

Measured before this design, on about 5,000 characters:

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Tokenizing, German and English | under 1 ms |
| Full analysis, German and English | 47 to 52 ms |
| Tokenizing, Japanese (as today) | 13 ms |
| One pass over the document for all registered terms | 0.02 to 0.03 ms |
| Analysing one term for registration | 0.17 ms |
| Confirming one compound candidate | 0.3 ms |

Rules:

- A check in which no selected domain has a qualifying term in the
  language does not request an analysis and runs no additional pass. A
  test asserts that the analysis is not requested.
- The analysis is requested at most once per request, and not at all when
  the rules checker has already produced it.
- Term base forms come from the cache after the first check.

Measured again and recorded in the first pull request and in the logbook:

- Check latency for a 5,000-character document in German and English,
  with 0, 10 and 1,000 switched-on terms, each with and without the rules
  checker.
- Runtime of the backend test suite, locally and in CI, before and after.
  An increase of more than 10 % needs a reason in the pull request.

## 10. Known limits, documented for the writer

- Phrases whose words are reordered or separated, and German separable
  verbs in separated position, are not found.
- The pipelines miss some forms. Measured: the small German model leaves
  "speicherten" unchanged; the French one leaves the verb "porte"
  unchanged. The writer can add such a form as a term of its own, and the
  example sentence test shows whether a given form is found.
- Compounds with two valid readings and derivations with a prefix
  ("Aufgabe" for "Gabe") can be reported wrongly, and a compound whose
  last part changes its vowel in the plural can be missed (6.5).
- The part-of-speech limit depends on the tagger being right in the
  sentence.

`docs/backend-architecture.md` describes these.

## 11. Calls made by the author

These were not discussed explicitly. Each can be changed without affecting
the rest.

1. **Compounds are confirmed with the spelling dictionary, not with
   CharSplit**, which the issue names (4.2). Reasons: it was more accurate
   on both test lists, needs no new dependency, and CharSplit cannot be
   installed cleanly from PyPI.
2. **No list of excluded prefixes in the compound pass** (6.5). It would
   remove false hits such as "Aufgabe" and lose wanted ones such as
   "Unterordner".
3. **The sidebar note and "Suggest fix" for base-form suggestions are in
   the first pull request** (section 12), not in the second as agreed for
   "replacements". Otherwise the first one would ship a misleading
   one-click replacement.
4. **Between findings of equal severity, the earlier pass wins** (6.3).
5. **A replacement without a stored part of speech takes that of the
   hit** (7.1).
6. **LemmInflect is a candidate, not a decision** (7.2). It was not
   installed for this design.
7. **A finding's span grows by one neighbouring word** when the article
   (English) or the following particle (Japanese) has to change with the
   replacement. The alternative is to decline in these cases.
8. **Case rule for inflected hits of a case-sensitive term** (6.3).
9. **Tolerance between noun and proper noun, verb and auxiliary, and
   German adjective and adverb** in the part-of-speech limit (6.3).
10. **A hit that equals a preferred or admitted term of the domain is not
    reported** (6.3).
11. **Three base-form sequences per term** (6.3), so that a term whose
    lone analysis differs from its analysis in a sentence is still found.
12. **Term base forms are cached in memory**, not stored in a column.
13. **Switch values are stored for every language and status** and take
    effect only where supported (section 5).
14. **The 95 % threshold on 200 terms** for German and Italian (8.3).
15. **The term check is an endpoint of its own**, stateless, and advice
    only.
16. **The live test of the existing LLM prompt is a step of the first
    pull request** (7.5), not part of this design.
